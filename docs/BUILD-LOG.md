# Build log

Every place the build departs from the approved docs (BRIEF, PRD, TECHNICAL-PLAN, DESIGN-SYSTEM, DECISIONS), with the reason. Built 2026-10-05.

The owner's brief for the build: keep everything the PRD specifies (the five routes, the 30 recipes and their filters, the servings stepper, the hand-rolled SVG donut and rings, the weekly board, the calculator), but make NutriPlan a tool real people open every week: on Sunday evening to plan, in the supermarket with the list on their phone, in the kitchen with their hands busy, and to eat to a calorie or protein target without spreadsheets. Break the BRIEF's non-goals wherever that makes it more useful. Those reversals come first, then the smaller design and engineering departures.

## Performance (owner: "the first paint must come from static HTML")

| # | Spec says | Build does | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 28 | D1/D3/D10: a client-rendered SPA with a catch-all rewrite | **Every page is prerendered**, in both languages: a second Vite build (`--ssr src/entry-server.jsx`) renders each route through `StaticRouter`, and `scripts/prerender.mjs` writes `dist/<route>/index.html` (72 pages: 36 routes × 2 languages, all 30 recipes included) plus `404.html`, with the CSS **inlined**, the page's own chunk and fonts preloaded, hreflang alternates, per-page titles, descriptions and Open Graph images, a sitemap, and the hero photo preloaded on Home. The client **hydrates** (`hydrateRoot`); pages are lazy chunks inside `<Suspense>`. No SPA rewrite: Vercel serves the static pages and `404.html` for anything else, where the router renders the not-found state or redirects home | Pulse scored 72–79 because nothing painted before the bundle ran; Serenity reached 99–100 this way. Saved data is read with `useSyncExternalStore`, so hydration matches the HTML and the saved plan appears right after. Two traps found: React 19's progressive chunking outlined big Suspense boundaries behind an inline reveal script (turned off with `progressiveChunkSize`), and awaiting the page chunk at the top level of the entry deadlocked (the chunk imports the entry). Both have regression tests. |
| 29 | TECHNICAL-PLAN §6: entrances, hover lift, rings, numbers, menu in Motion | Hero entrance, scroll reveals, hover lifts, chip taps and the mobile menu are **CSS** (they run from the static HTML); rings and donut fill with CSS transitions on `stroke-dashoffset`, armed only if the panel loads below the fold; numbers count with a small rAF tween. **Motion** does what it's needed for: the recipe grid's layout reflow, the planner cards' enter/exit and Clear-day stagger, through `LazyMotion` with features loaded after hydration | Motion stays out of the first paint. Until its features arrive, the grid and the board render plainly (an exit animation that can't run would otherwise hold back the new card: found and fixed, with a regression test). Everything honours `prefers-reduced-motion`. |
| 30 | D9: WebP, 1200×900 per recipe | **AVIF and WebP** at 160/480/800/1200 px (hero 640–1600) generated by `npm run images` (sharp) from the originals listed in `scripts/image-sources.json`, served with `<picture>`, `srcset`/`sizes`, explicit dimensions, lazy below the fold, and the hero (Home) and each recipe's photo (its page) eager with `fetchpriority="high"` and a preload in the head | Smaller files for phones, sharper ones for big screens, and the LCP image fetched with the HTML. |
| 31 | BRIEF criterion 4: initial JS ≤ 180 KB gz | `scripts/check-bundle.mjs` measures the real first load of **each page** (the entry plus the page's preloaded chunks, and the Arabic text on `/ar` pages) and fails the build above 180 KB, or if a page stops preloading its own chunk | Stricter than one number for the whole app. |

## Not verified

- Screen Wake Lock, the chime with the screen locked, vibration and the iOS share sheet were not tested on physical phones. Wake Lock is supported in current Chrome, Edge and Safari (16.4+); where it isn't, cook mode says so.
- Nutrition values are careful estimates for planning (per-serving macros cross-checked against the 4/4/9 rule), not laboratory figures; the app says so.
